Story // Scott “Scotty” Horan

The door stayed open.

I grew up in Buffalo, New York. I lived in Tahoe for a while. I’ve been in San Francisco since 2018. I tell you this not as geography but as trajectory. I didn’t arrive here already knowing who I was or what I made. I arrived still figuring it out.

I’m a hairstylist. That’s been my work and my craft for years: a career spent developing an eye for tone, contrast, and transformation, with my hands on other people’s heads. And for most of that time, I would have told you I was creative but not an artist. I said it so often it became a wall.

About two years ago, something shifted. Journaling became a way of processing a period of deep personal reconstruction. Shadow work, the kind where you stop running from the parts of yourself you’ve buried. Writing became a container, and then the container got too small. I picked up paint because I needed to see something bigger than words. I needed the whole picture.

I have no formal training. What I have is painting from instinct, from color memory, from observation, from discipline, and the willingness to keep learning. The work is real, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty.

“I didn’t get the residency. But applying made me let the work be seen. That changed something. I am not closing that door again.”

Applying to Art + Water in 2026 was the first time I put the work in front of people who didn’t know me. That vulnerability opened a door, and the door is still open. This site is what walking through it looks like.
