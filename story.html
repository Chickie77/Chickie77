Artist Statement

Visual artist working in paint, mixed media, and non-traditional surfaces.

My practice centers on material intelligence, treating color and substrate as active participants. Wood rounds, sketchbook spreads, and found panels function not as backdrops but as collaborators. Through a recurring symbolic language of faces, eyes, halos, and botanical forms, I explore themes of identity, survival, observation, and queer individuation.

The work is not decorative but structural: high-contrast emotional color, nervous layering of line, and frames within frames that feel less composed than inhabited.

My Story

I grew up in Buffalo, New York. I lived in Tahoe for a while. I’ve been in San Francisco since 2018. I tell you this not as geography but as trajectory, because I didn’t arrive here already knowing who I was or what I made. I arrived still figuring it out.

I’m a hairstylist. That’s been my work and my craft for years. And for most of that time, I would have told you I was creative but not an artist. I said it so often it became a wall. I believed it.

About two years ago, something shifted. I started journaling seriously and regularly as a way of processing a period of deep personal reconstruction. Shadow work, the Jungian kind, where you stop running from the parts of yourself you’ve buried. Writing became a container, and then the container got too small. I picked up paint because I needed to see something bigger than words. I needed the whole picture.

I still find it uncomfortable to call myself an artist. I’m saying it anyway, because the work is real, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty. I have no formal training. What I have is two years of painting from instinct, from color memory, from emotional intensity, from the same intuition about tone and contrast and transformation that I’ve spent a career developing with my hands on other people’s heads. Hair taught me color. Life taught me shadow. Painting gave both somewhere to go.
