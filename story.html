Artist Statement

Paint, mixed media, and non-traditional surfaces: canvas, paper, sketchbooks, wood rounds, whatever is in reach.

Honestly, I don’t really know what the hell I’m doing. I just know I spent half my life (hoping there’s another half) letting a ceramic castle decide who I was. I made it in tenth grade art class: a castle in ruins, overgrown with ivy, with a little river running through it. It didn’t get the validation I was hoping for. Add to that the fact that I couldn’t draw, and nobody ever told me drawing was something you could learn. I thought you either could or you couldn’t.

What I do know is that I love color and shapes. I love drawing, even when it’s scribbled lines of chaos. If I keep going, something comes around. The intention kicks in. And then it can, and often does, leave again when I overwork it. I’m finding my way through that.

I’m learning my voice, and learning that it doesn’t have to fill every part of the drawing or the painting. The white, the negative space, can be a sense of control in a world full of chaos, to borrow a line from Clueless.

I just like to make stuff, and to imagine I might get to live the life I always felt was mine. Not the persona I learned would be best received by the people around me. I don’t recognize that person anymore. I am not that person.

That metamorphosis is what the color, the paint, the drawing, all of it, is really about.

It is Becoming.

My Story

I grew up in Buffalo, New York. I lived in Tahoe for a while. I’ve been in San Francisco since 2018. I tell you this not as a geography lesson, but to show the trajectory. I didn’t arrive here already knowing who I was or what I made. I arrived still figuring it out.

I’m a hairstylist. That’s been my work and my craft for years. And for most of that time, I would have told you I was creative but not an artist. I said it so often it became a wall. I believed it.

In 2024, something shifted. I started journaling seriously as a way of processing a period of deep personal reconstruction. Shadow work, the Jungian kind, where you stop running from the parts of yourself you’ve buried. Writing became a container, and then the container got too small. I picked up paint because I needed to see something bigger than words. I needed the whole picture.

I still find it uncomfortable to call myself an artist. I’m saying it anyway, because the work is real, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty. I have no formal training. What I have is a few years of painting from instinct, color memory, and emotional intensity. It’s the same feel for tone, contrast, and transformation I’ve spent a career building with my hands on other people’s heads. Hair taught me color. Life taught me shadow. Painting gave both somewhere to go.
